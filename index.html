About

A strategic advisor at the intersection of law, technology, and government.

Alla Seiffert is a Washington, DC-based public policy executive and attorney who has spent her career translating fast-moving technology into policy government can act on. As Director of US Regulated Industries and Federal Public Sector Public Policy at Amazon Web Services, she tracks the congressional and regulatory decisions that shape how the federal government buys and adopts cloud technology, and turns that analysis into strategy for AWS and its customers — including guidance through merger and acquisition activity.

Before AWS, she directed cloud policy for the Internet Association, representing member companies before Congress and federal and state agencies on public-sector and financial-services issues. Earlier, inside government itself, she served as acting director of GSA's Office of Acquisition within the Technology Transformation Service, and as a product manager at 18F — the team of technologists and designers built to modernize how federal agencies deliver digital services.

"The free and open internet still matters. I love helping make technology easier — and safer — to adopt across highly regulated sectors."

She holds a J.D. from the University of Arizona's James E. Rogers College of Law and is a member of the Arizona State Bar. She was named a FedScoop 50 honoree in 2018 and a Federal 100 honoree in 2026 for her work modernizing how government buys and governs technology.

Public sector, regulated industries, and federal cloud policy

Alla chairs the Government Affairs Committee for the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), a Linux Foundation initiative formed with founding contributions from Anthropic, Block, and OpenAI to govern open, interoperable standards for agentic AI — including the Model Context Protocol, goose, and AGENTS.md.

The role extends a career-long pattern: at Booz Allen Hamilton, she supervised the open-source Drupal redesign of ise.gov for the Office of the Director of National Intelligence; at 18F, she owned the backlog for an in-house, agile-built procurement platform. In each case, the throughline is the same: open standards make it easier, and safer, for governments and regulated sectors to move fast.
